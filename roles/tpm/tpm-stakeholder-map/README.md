# Stakeholder Map - quick guide

## What it's for
Systematically identifies everyone who does the work, is affected by it, influences it, or supports a program - and prioritizes how much attention each of them needs, using power and interest.

## How to trigger it
- Plain English: "map the stakeholders for the Caregiver Access program"
- Name it directly: "use tpm-stakeholder-map to identify who's involved in X"
- Slash command: `/tpm-stakeholder-map`

## What you'll be asked for
- The program name (required)
- Real stakeholders once the starter draft is generated - who they are, what they actually said their objective is (not a guess), and how much power/interest they have

## What happens, step by step
1. Reads the Program Brief for the problem, goals, and scope, so discovery is scoped to what the program actually touches.
2. Generates a starter map with standard categories already filled in (customers, dependent teams, systems, sponsors, influencers, support), instead of a blank page.
3. You replace the starter rows with the program's real stakeholders, one category at a time.
4. Capture each stakeholder's actual objective from a real conversation, not an assumption based on their role.
5. Plots each stakeholder on a power/interest grid, sorting them into one of four groups: Manage Closely, Keep Satisfied, Keep Informed, or Monitor.
6. Sets an engagement approach per group (direct involvement, periodic updates, broadcast updates, or occasional checks).
7. Flags any high-power/high-interest stakeholder who isn't engaged yet - that's the gap that causes late surprises.
8. Revisits the map as the program evolves - stakeholders move between groups as scope or circumstances change.

## What you get
A table: Stakeholder/Role, Category, Objective, Power, Interest, Priority Group, Engagement Approach - plus a reference explaining what each priority group means.

## Works well with
- Pulls from: `tpm-program-brief` (problem, scope, initial stakeholder names)
- Feeds: `tpm-raci` and `tpm-contested-decisions` (this roster is exactly who gets assigned ownership roles downstream)
- This skill identifies and prioritizes stakeholders; it doesn't assign accountability - that's RACI's or Contested Decisions' job
