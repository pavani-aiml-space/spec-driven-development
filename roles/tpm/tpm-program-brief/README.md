# Program Brief - quick guide

## What it's for
Gives everyone touching a new program the same shared understanding of what it is, why it matters, and what's in or out of scope - short enough that people actually read it.

## How to trigger it
- Plain English: "write a program brief for the Caregiver Access program"
- Name it directly: "use tpm-program-brief to frame this new initiative"
- Slash command: `/tpm-program-brief`

## What you'll be asked for
- The program name (required)
- The problem this program solves and what success looks like, in measurable terms
- What's explicitly in scope and out of scope
- Sponsor, dependent teams, and who needs to be consulted
- Key milestones and target dates
- Known risks and dependencies

## What happens, step by step
1. Generates a blank starter document with section prompts already in place, instead of a blank page.
2. You state the problem and goal - why this program exists and what success looks like, concretely.
3. You define what's in scope and, just as importantly, what's explicitly out of scope.
4. You name the sponsor, dependent teams, and who needs to be consulted.
5. You lay out key milestones and dates, being honest about what's uncertain.
6. You surface known risks and dependencies up front, not buried.
7. Keep the whole thing to one page - if it doesn't fit, the scope is probably still too broad.

## What you get
A one-page brief: problem/goal, in/out of scope, stakeholders, timeline/milestones, key risks and dependencies.

## Works well with
- Feeds: `tpm-stakeholder-map` (systematic stakeholder discovery, if the one-line list here isn't enough), `tpm-risk-register` (ongoing risk tracking beyond this brief's summary list), `tpm-raci` (deliverables and initial stakeholder names)
- Usually the very first artifact created when a new program kicks off
